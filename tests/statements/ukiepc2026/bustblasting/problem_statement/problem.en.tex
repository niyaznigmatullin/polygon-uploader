\problemname{Bust Blasting}

Charlie the CNC miller is a cad with a capital `C'. He is going to clone a
valuable statue of a famous voxel-based video game character, and you are going
to help him.

The voxel shape is defined by three photos Charlie took, which have been traced
into the form of simple, axis-aligned (but potentially concave) polygons
representing front ($xy$), side ($zy$), and top ($xz$) views of the statue
respectively.

The meaning of ``axis-aligned'' here is that every edge of every polygon is
parallel with one of the $X$/$Y$/$Z$ axes.

Any 3-dimensional point not contained by all three
polygon projections will be \emph{blasted out of existence} leaving only the true
form, which may consist of multiple pieces or no pieces at all.

\begin{figure}[!h]
\centering
\includegraphics[height=0.3\textwidth]{bust.pdf}
\caption{Illustration of a complex statue.}
\end{figure}

Calculate the volume of the final statue.

\section*{Input}

The input consists of three lines of input, each giving one view as a two-dimensional, axis-aligned
polygon in anti-clockwise order, with integer coordinates measured in centimetres.

\begin{itemize}
  \item First, the number of points, $n_i$ ($4 \le n \le 100$).
  \item Thereafter, $n_i$ pairs of integers $u_{ij}$ and $v_{ij}$ for all $1 \le j \le n_i$
        ($-10\,000 \le u,v \le 10\,000$).
\end{itemize}

It is guaranteed that for all adjacent vertices $a$ and $b$, $u_{ia} = u_{ib}$ or $v_{ia} = v_{ib}$.
No three consecutive vertices are collinear.

\section*{Output}

Output the integer total volume of the resulting shape(s) in cubic centimetres.
