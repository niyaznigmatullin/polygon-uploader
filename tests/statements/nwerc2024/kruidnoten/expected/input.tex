
    The input consists of:
    \begin{itemize}
    \item One line with three integers $n$, $m$, and $k$ ($2\leq n\leq 2 \cdot 10^5$, $1\leq m\leq 2 \cdot 10^5$, $1\leq k\leq n$), the number of
    intersections, cycleways, and stores that may sell kruidnoten.
    \item $m$ lines, each with three integers $i$, $j$, and $\ell$ ($1\leq i, j\leq n$, $i \neq j$, $1 \leq \ell \leq 10^6$), indicating that the intersections $i$ and $j$ are connected by a cycleway of length $\ell$.
    It is guaranteed that every pair of intersections is connected by at most one cycleway.
    Further, it is possible to get from every intersection to every other intersection by bike.
    \item $k$ lines, each with one integer $i$ ($1\leq i \leq n$) and one floating-point number $p$ ($0 < p \leq 1$ with at most four digits after the decimal point), indicating that there is a store located at intersection $i$, and that the probability that it still has kruidnoten is $p$.
    It is guaranteed that there is at most one kruidnoten store at each intersection.
    \end{itemize}
